How were these sounds made?

These nine steps were exported from the application, changing phonation and fundamental frequency together. Analysis uses Praat / Parselmouth over 40–300 Hz, with cycle-energy and output-amplitude matching enabled. The original median F0 values are approximately 128 Hz and 94 Hz. The processed median F0 values are approximately 127, 122, 118, 112, 106, 102, 100, 97 and 94 Hz.

The nine steps represent processing ratios; perceived differences need not be equally spaced. Steps 1 and 9 are processed results too, and can be compared with the two originals above.

How much sound can be recovered from a spectrogram image? Select its spectral region, calibrate the axes, then reconstruct it using GL, a trained 80-band Mel restoration model and a vocoder. These three clips are approximate reconstructions from images.

One image. A greeting.

Smith (2007) · Fig. 8.10 ↗

Spectrogram-to-audio
Model training in progress✦

Shaozi has been exploring better spectrogram-to-audio reconstruction. A small model estimates the STFT window length and dynamic range that may have produced an image, using its time and frequency calibration. These estimates guide the reconstruction parameters before recovering sound.

For phase reconstruction, Shaozi follows Naofumi Aoki's research on short-window spectrograms: sum along frequency, extract peak pulses from the temporal projection, construct an excitation signal, then use its phase to initialize Griffin–Lim. This supplies a starting point for iteration. Perraudin and colleagues' momentum acceleration also uses changes between adjacent iterations to help phase estimates approach the target faster. The toolbox currently uses 0.99 as its momentum coefficient, which generally speeds convergence.

After initial reconstruction, the next step is Mel restoration. Convert the sound to a Mel spectrogram and train a conditional NAFNet against target Mel spectrograms to repair spectral errors, aiming for structures closer to natural speech. Feed the restored Mel spectrogram to the pretrained BigVGAN vocoder to generate the final waveform. The vocoder uses learned speech regularities to synthesize the restored spectrum, somewhat like the role of learned models in denoising or echo cancellation.

Work is still in progress. Compare five stages below: basic GL, momentum acceleration, parameter prediction, Aoki initialization and Mel restoration.

How was this comparison made?

All five routes share the same image, time/frequency calibration and Hann window. Every GL route uses 1.5 ms hop size and 32 iterations. The following steps add one change at a time:

  1. Basic GL: Fixed 10 ms window,60 dB dynamic range, random-phase initialization and momentum 0。
  2. Momentum GL: Keep the first route's parameters and initial random phase; change only momentum to 0.99。
  3. Predicted parameters + momentum GL: Keep momentum at 0.99 and random phase; use the model's predicted window length and dynamic range, withwindow length rounded to whole milliseconds。
  4. Predicted parameters + Aoki initialization + momentum GL: Keep the third route's predicted parameters and momentum 0.99, then add Aoki pulse initialization。
  5. Mel restoration and vocoder:Read the exact audio exported by the fourth route, then apply 80-band NAFNet Mel restorationand BigVGAN vocoder。

The first three routes retain their fixed audio. During playback, all five are attenuated to the same RMS level.
